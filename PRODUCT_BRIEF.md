# Product Brief

## One-sentence concept

An interactive digital museum that accompanies a live history presentation and lets the audience explore the exhibition on their phones.

## Why this project exists

Slides and videos often leave the audience as passive viewers. This website turns a presentation into a visit: while the presenter speaks, each visitor can open a map, story, archaeological find, or source on their phone. The site should also be useful without a presenter.

## Audience

- People attending a live presentation, primarily using phones.
- Students and general visitors interested in Azerbaijani history and the history of winemaking.
- Visitors who return later to explore the exhibition independently.

## Core visitor journey

1. A visitor scans a QR code and opens the site without creating an account.
2. They choose a self-guided visit or follow the presentation.
3. They explore the map, timeline, short stories, and interactive 3D exhibits.
4. They can inspect the evidence level and sources behind historical claims.
5. They can save or share a link to an exhibition section.

## Product principles

- Design for phones first.
- Keep 3D motion under the visitor’s control; avoid distracting autoplay.
- Present historical claims with their evidence and sources.
- Make the first explanation concise, with optional deeper detail.
- Keep the rest of the site usable if 3D is slow or unavailable.
- Consider accessibility in text, controls, contrast, and motion.
- Use Azerbaijani for the first interface; keep the content structure ready for later translation.
- Treat the subject as history and culture, not as encouragement to consume alcohol.

## First release

**In scope:** welcome screen, timeline, interactive map, direct links for presentation sections, source cards, at least one 3D exhibit, and a static visual alternative for devices without working 3D.

**Out of scope for the first release:** a fully explorable 3D city, user accounts, social features, and historical claims without sources.

## Signs of success

The QR code opens quickly; visitors can find a section easily; they explore at least one map point and exhibit; they can find the source for a claim; the site does not distract from the live presentation.
