# Historical Research and Evidence Notes

This document is not a verified final history. It tracks initial claims from the Perplexity exports and what must be checked before publishing them in the exhibition.

## Evidence levels

- **Strong:** a primary archaeological report or independent scholarly study directly supports the claim.
- **Qualified:** credible research exists, but uncertainty remains about dating or interpretation.
- **Preliminary:** the claim appears mainly in secondary, tourism, or news sources; primary evidence is still needed.
- **Unverified:** available sources do not directly establish the claim.

## Initial claim register

| Claim | Initial status | Cautious exhibition wording | What to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finds at Georgia’s Shulaveri–Gadachrili sites indicate chemical traces of grape wine around 6000–5800 BCE. | Strong | “Some of the earliest known biomolecular evidence for wine was found here.” | Methods, samples, dating, and limits in the PNAS paper. |
| Hajji Firuz Tepe and Areni-1 are important early winemaking examples. | Qualified | Give each find’s date and evidence type separately. | Primary excavation reports and scientific analyses. |
| Wine was produced near the Arpachay River about 6,000 years ago. | Preliminary | “Some local sources interpret the finds this way; the primary report is being checked.” | Excavation report, stratigraphy, and residue analysis. |
| Finds at Göygöl, Kültepe, Qarabulaq, and Gələcik indicate winemaking. | Preliminary/qualified | Name each find; do not label all of them direct evidence of wine. | Site-specific reports and residue analyses. |
| Qabala had a wine cellar dated to the 1st–3rd centuries CE. | Preliminary | “Interpreted as a storage or production site connected with wine.” | Primary archaeological publication and building function. |
| Strabo explicitly mentioned Azerbaijani wine. | Unverified | Do not present *Geography* 11.14.4’s description of fertile land as a direct reference to wine. | Greek text and a reliable translation. |
| The spread of Islam reduced winemaking in Azerbaijan. | Preliminary | Distinguish rules on public consumption from viticulture and production. | Direct written sources for specific places and periods. |
| German settlers developed modern commercial winemaking in Göygöl in the 19th century; 1860 is commonly cited. | Qualified | “Göygöl became a major center of modern commercial winemaking in the 19th century.” | Archives, winery history, and the scope of “first.” |
| The 1985 campaign seriously damaged vineyards and the industry. | Qualified | Avoid specific figures unless reliable statistics support them. | Official vineyard-area and production series. |
| Madrasa and Bayan Shira are leading local wine grapes. | Qualified | “Two of Azerbaijan’s best-known local wine grapes.” | Ampelographic catalogs and scholarly or genetic studies. |

## Record for every published fact

Record the exact claim; source title and link; author and date; page or section; source type; whether it directly supports the claim; dating method; uncertainty; and the cautious wording intended for the exhibit.

A large number of Perplexity citations does not by itself verify a claim. Read the cited source and check whether a secondary article represents the primary research accurately.
